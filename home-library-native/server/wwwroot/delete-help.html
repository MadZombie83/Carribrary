Delete your Carribrary account and data

Your library is hosted by its owner. The Carribrary developer does not hold your household library database.

  1. Sign in to this library in your browser. No Android app installation is needed.
  2. Open Account → Data & privacy → My data & privacy → Delete my account.
  3. Review account-only versus account-and-linked-information deletion. The preview shows the affected records.
  4. Enter your current password and type the sign-in username shown in the deletion preview (not your reader/display name) to confirm. This ends your sign-ins, resets and private reading link access.

The borrower confirmation field accepts capitalization differences, surrounding spaces and one optional leading @. Your password is unchanged and must match exactly. Switching deletion scope clears the confirmation and requires a fresh preview.

Both deletion choices remove the current encrypted private vault. Export your encrypted personal backup first if you want to keep it.

Full information deletion includes linked reading records, notes, ratings, returned-loan history and linked requests. Associated holds are released. Books remain in the catalog. Active physical loans must be resolved first for full information deletion; deleting the sign-in account alone remains possible while books are outstanding and retains library records. Contact your librarian to arrange returns and removal of retained records. Shared-contact guest requests cannot be attributed automatically.

Older private backups, exported files, emails and other devices' offline copies may contain earlier data. Ask the library owner about their retention and deletion practices; they control stored backups. Clearing current history does not remove those copies. There is no automatic remote wipe of other devices.

If you cannot sign in, contact your library owner for identity verification and account/data deletion. For Carribrary software support, email chezburbermerch@gmail.com. Do not email passwords or API keys. Software support may help you locate the correct deletion route but cannot delete a self-hosted database it does not operate.

Encrypted personal lists

Your current private-list protection

Borrower personal TBR/reading records, ratings, notes and private wishlists are encrypted in the borrower’s browser with a separate private passphrase and a random recovery code. These secrets and raw private-list encryption keys are not sent to Carribrary Server or saved in browser storage by Carribrary. The server stores an account-bound encrypted document. Administrative state, phone caches and library exports exclude borrower personal reading. Unlinked household reading profiles remain admin-managed and visible to paired admins. Shelf locations and the household Books to buy acquisition list are admin-only and excluded from borrower/public projections.

Requests/messages intentionally sent to the librarian, loan/due-date history, account identity and service metadata remain operational server records. Login password resets do not decrypt a private list. Losing both the private passphrase and recovery code makes the personal contents unrecoverable. Owners can export/restore an encrypted personal backup with their own secret. Both account-deletion scopes remove the current encrypted vault; export it first if desired. Administrative library JSON/scheduled library snapshots are not backups of borrower vaults or credentials; a complete portable server backup or the borrower’s encrypted personal export is needed.

Legacy plaintext reading records move into encryption during explicit borrower setup: ciphertext is stored before the current plaintext/associated receipts are erased. Previous backups, exports and device copies may still contain older unencrypted information. No retroactive encryption or remote wipe is promised. The server delivers the browser application, so an actively malicious or compromised host could modify it to capture future secrets; this feature is not protection from a malicious app host and has not received an independent security audit.

Windows recovery points can retain historical private data after account deletion. The owner removes these separately with Update and recovery → Delete recovery points, which also removes rollback ability. Ordinary backup cleanup and account deletion do not remove these administrator-protected points or external copies.
