Your library
Carribrary is a phone companion for a library server you or your household operate. Books, borrower names and optional contact details, loans, due dates and borrowing requests are stored on that server. Paired phones can view and administer that library. The app sends library changes to your server over an encrypted connection and verifies the paired server identity. Device names and cryptographic pairing credentials identify approved phones. The publisher does not operate a hosted library service in this version.

On your phone
The app keeps an encrypted library cache for viewing while offline, encrypted pending changes for retrying, and preferences and pairing information. Private cryptographic keys are held in Android Keystore. Camera access is optional and requested when scanning. Barcode and QR recognition runs on the phone; camera frames are not uploaded by Carribrary. You can type codes instead. Carribrary does not read your address book or automatically send texts or emails.

External services
Book-detail lookups send the ISBN to Open Library. When you search for a cover, the app sends the ISBN and book title/author to your library server. The server queries Open Library and, when a matching item is available, Internet Archive. If the household configures a Google Books API key, Google Books is an additional fallback when no cover is found. Google receives the ISBN, book title and author used for cover searches, and the server’s IP address; the key is stored protected on the server and excluded from library exports. Google-sourced images and book links contact Google from the viewing device. Borrower information and loan history are not sent to these providers. Cover images are fetched from the selected provider or the cover URL stored with a book. Providers receive the requested URL and normal network information, including the IP address of the server for server lookups and of the phone for image requests. Their policies apply to these requests.
Google ML Kit provides barcode recognition. It can report device/app information, installation identifiers, performance and scanner events to Google for diagnostics and usage analytics. Carribrary adds no advertising or separate analytics service. See Google's ML Kit disclosure and privacy information at https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure and https://policies.google.com/privacy .

Sharing you choose
A public book-list link exposes catalog details and availability to people with the link. Borrower contacts and private loan history are not shown on that list. Requesters may send a name, contact information and message to the household server through its request form. Library administrators can review those requests. You can disable public sharing.
Preparing a borrowing receipt hands the selected recipient and receipt text to your chosen messaging/email app; you decide whether to send it. That service's policies apply. Exporting a backup or book list saves or shares it at your direction. JSON backups include private borrower and loan details; protect them and share them only as intended.

Retention and deletion
The household controls the server and its backups. Removing a borrower or book with history may archive it to preserve loan records rather than erase that history. Ask the household administrator about removing retained records and backups. Forgetting the phone connection removes its pairing and encrypted library/pending/share caches; it does not delete the server's library. Clearing Android app storage or uninstalling removes app-local data, but previously exported files remain until you delete them. Revoking a phone on the server stops further access. The publisher cannot delete a library hosted on your computer. Google and other service providers apply their own retention policies.

Support and changes
Do not send borrower records, private pairing QR codes or backups when requesting support unless you deliberately choose to share them. This policy will be updated if the app's data handling changes. Publisher contact details and a public policy URL must be supplied before this draft becomes a production policy.

Reading tracker and personal links
Household reading profiles, status, dates, ratings and notes are stored on the household server and included in its backups and paired-phone cache. A profile may be linked to a borrower. Reading status does not change loans automatically. A separate personal reading link or QR gives its holder access to that profile’s reading entries, linked contact information, own borrowing requests and the book catalog. Holders can edit only that profile’s reading entries and submit requests for admin approval. The link is an access credential: anyone holding it can use it. Administrators can replace or revoke it. Shared public catalog links do not expose reading profiles, ratings or notes. Personal-link credentials are stored protected on the server, separately from library exports. The household and paired admins can view all profiles; these are not individually password-protected accounts.

Borrower sign-in accounts
Visitors can register a username, password, name and optional contact details through an enabled shared library page. The household server stores salted password hashes, approvals, account-to-reader/borrower links and hashed session/recovery tokens in a separate private credential database. Passwords are not stored in plaintext or included in library exports. Account approval and explicit linking are required to access existing borrowing history. Approved sessions can view only their own loan and reading history and submit requests. The browser uses Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict session cookies lasting up to one day. Password recovery is a librarian-issued, one-use link expiring in 30 minutes; no automated email verification or recovery provider is used. Resetting a password ends existing sessions. Blocking an account ends its sessions and revokes its linked personal reading QR. Accounts and session data stay on the household server; the publisher does not host sign-ins. The household controls retention and access.


Optional due-date reminders send the borrower name, book details and due date through the library owner’s configured email provider, only after the librarian records permission. SMTP credentials are encrypted in the private server folder and excluded from library JSON exports. Delivery-attempt metadata is stored separately. Deleted books/borrowers remain recoverable in the Recycle Bin until the owner removes that recovery entry; records needed for loan/reading history remain archived. Windows pre-update safety copies contain the complete private database and key material and must be kept private.


Account deletion is available through My data & privacy in the borrower browser app and through the library owner in Borrower accounts. Account-only deletion removes current sign-in credentials/session/reset records and revokes linked personal reading QR access while retaining operational library records; the current encrypted personal vault is removed. Full personal-information deletion additionally removes explicitly linked/selected borrower and reading profiles, reading notes, requests and returned-loan history; active books must first be returned. Other guest requests sharing contact details are deleted only if selected. This bypasses the Recycle Bin and does not make a pre-deletion backup. The owner can optionally delete all installation-managed automatic/safety backups; exported copies, other backups, emails and offline caches may retain older information. New registration with a deleted username starts pending without inherited access. This is logical application deletion, not guaranteed forensic disk erasure.


The optional borrower Home screen web app stores only the last shared catalog path in browser local storage. It uses the existing secure account cookie for sign-in, where applicable. Its network-only service worker does not cache borrower profiles, contacts, loans, notes, credentials or API responses for offline use. Installing does not grant extra access. Chrome/browser-managed installed-app metadata and previously exported/offline copies remain subject to the browser/device and owner controls.

Optional PWA additions in 2.0.23: Browser admin pairing uses a separate protected, expiring, revocable session. The iPhone and borrower PWAs do not cache private API responses for offline use. Their browser stores normal sign-in cookies, public name/color branding and the last public catalog path. Custom library icons and book descriptions are public. On-demand descriptions use Open Library and, when configured, Google Books with only book identifiers. Web Push is off until the user opts in and grants browser permission. Subscription endpoints/keys and VAPID keys are protected on the server. Apple/Google/Mozilla/Microsoft push services see endpoints and timing; empty push messages contain no book titles, contact info or reading notes. Workers display generic reminders or request alerts. Unsubscribe, sign-out, account block/deletion or browser revocation stops the corresponding subscriptions. Native Android supports optional periodic request alerts, distinct from PWA Web Push. Email reminders and tap-to-send texts remain separate. Browser/OS permission and delivery settings apply.

Android native request alerts: Manage library → Enable background request alerts, then grant notification permission. Android polls the paired server about every 15 minutes, subject to battery/OS scheduling; these are not instant push. Existing pending requests are the initial baseline. Generic alerts open Requests; disable or Forget connection cancels them. No PWA or Firebase account is needed; only request-ID hashes are kept for deduplication.


2026-10-20 update — borrower data controls
Borrowers can sign in and use My data & privacy to clear completed history or delete their own sign-in account and linked information after a preview, password check and typed username confirmation. The librarian has the same administrative deletion tools. Full information deletion requires resolving outstanding physical loans; sign-in deletion alone remains available and retains library records, which is disclosed before confirmation. Ask the library owner to resolve retained records and private backup copies. No automatic remote wipe of disconnected devices is promised. /account/delete-help on the library HTTPS address provides the browser-only path and support instructions. The software publisher does not operate these household databases. The publisher’s public store deletion/help page still needs deployment and a verified URL before Play submission.
Completed-history clearing preserves active loans, holds and unfinished reading, uses a preview and typed confirmation, and does not create a new copy of erased history. It removes matching private idempotency receipt copies; older private backups, exports and messages are separate. Device cache clearing does not erase server data or pairing. This version does not implement whole-catalog borrower offline downloads.


Borrower personal encryption — 2.0.27
Borrower personal TBR/reading records, ratings, notes and private wishlists are encrypted in the borrower’s browser with a separate private passphrase and a random recovery code. These secrets and raw private-list encryption keys are not sent to Carribrary Server or saved in browser storage by Carribrary. The server stores an account-bound encrypted document. Administrative state, phone caches and library exports exclude borrower personal reading. Unlinked household reading profiles remain admin-managed and visible to paired admins. Shelf locations and the household Books to buy acquisition list are admin-only and excluded from borrower/public projections.
Requests/messages intentionally sent to the librarian, loan/due-date history, account identity and service metadata remain operational server records. Login password resets do not decrypt a private list. Losing both the private passphrase and recovery code makes the personal contents unrecoverable. Owners can export/restore an encrypted personal backup with their own secret. Both account-deletion scopes remove the current encrypted vault; export it first if desired. Administrative library JSON/scheduled library snapshots are not backups of borrower vaults or credentials; a complete stopped-server data-directory backup or the borrower’s encrypted export is needed.
Legacy plaintext reading records move into encryption during explicit borrower setup: ciphertext is stored before the current plaintext/associated receipts are erased. Previous backups, exports and device copies may still contain older unencrypted information. No retroactive encryption or remote wipe is promised. The server delivers the browser application, so an actively malicious or compromised host could modify it to capture future secrets; this feature is not protection from a malicious app host and has not received an independent security audit.

Windows historical recovery points are removed separately by the library owner using Update and recovery. Compatible program recovery retains current account and encrypted-vault data.

Optional backup verification sends only the selected complete encrypted archive and its password to your own paired server. The server temporarily decrypts it in protected isolated storage for integrity checks, then removes normal-operation temporary files. It does not restore live records. No archive or password is sent to the developer or a third-party verification service. A server crash can require administrator cleanup of protected temporary files.
